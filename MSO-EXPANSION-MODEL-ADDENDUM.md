## The Onevia Vision: From Single Practice to Healthcare Platform

The original DPC Clinic business plan focused on launching a single direct primary care practice in Missoula, Montana. While that remains the pilot program, **the true vision for Onevia is far more ambitious**: to build a healthcare management platform that empowers 10-20 physician-entrepreneurs to launch and operate their own independent direct care practices under the Onevia brand.

Onevia is not just a single clinic. **Onevia is a healthcare incubator and platform** that removes the operational, financial, and administrative barriers that prevent most physicians from launching independent practices.

---

## The Problem Onevia Solves for Physicians

Most physicians who are passionate about direct primary care face insurmountable barriers to launching independent practices:

### Financial Barriers:
- **Startup Capital:** $300,000-$400,000 required for buildout, equipment, EMR, and working capital
- **Real Estate:** Securing commercial clinic space requires credit, guarantees, and upfront capital
- **Cash Flow Risk:** 6-12 months to reach breakeven; physicians can't afford to defer income during ramp-up

### Operational Barriers:
- **Technology:** Choosing, implementing, and maintaining EMR, telehealth, patient portal, billing systems
- **Legal & Compliance:** Entity formation, HIPAA compliance, patient agreements, employment contracts
- **Administrative Burden:** HR, payroll, benefits, accounting, tax filing, vendor management

### Marketing Barriers:
- **Brand Development:** Building brand identity and community recognition from scratch
- **Patient Acquisition:** Marketing strategy, website, SEO, social media, community outreach
- **Credibility:** Patients trust established brands more than solo physician startups

### Strategic Barriers:
- **Isolation:** Solo physicians lack peer network, best practice sharing, and operational support
- **Limited Growth:** Traditional practices plateau at single location; scaling requires replicating entire infrastructure

**Result:** Only the most entrepreneurial, well-capitalized, and risk-tolerant physicians launch independent DPC practices. The vast majority remain trapped in employed positions within large health systems, despite burnout and dissatisfaction.

## The Onevia Solution: MSO as Healthcare Incubator

Onevia Management Services, Corp. (the "MSO") removes these barriers by functioning as a **turnkey healthcare incubator and platform** for physician-entrepreneurs.

### What Onevia MSO Provides:

#### 1. Startup Capital (via Commercial Loans)
- **Loan Amount:** $300,000-$400,000 per new practice
- **Purpose:** Clinic buildout, medical equipment, EMR implementation, initial working capital
- **Terms (Physician-Friendly Structure):**
  - **Years 1-2:** 0% interest (interest-free during practice launch and growth phase)
  - **Years 3-10:** 7% annual interest (fixed rate, amortized over remaining 8 years)
  - **Repayment:** 10-year total term with no prepayment penalty
  - **Security:** Personal guarantee from physician (standard for commercial loans)
- **Why This Matters:** 0% interest for 2 years saves physician $40k-$56k compared to immediate 7% rate
- **Advantage:** Physician doesn't need personal savings or bank approval; MSO finances launch with favorable terms

#### 2. Real Estate Advisory Services (MSO Advisory Model)
- **MSO Role:** Identifies potential clinic locations, evaluates demographics and market fit
- **Lease Negotiation:** MSO negotiates lease terms, rent, buildout allowances, and landlord concessions on physician's behalf
- **Buildout Coordination:** MSO manages contractor relationships, buildout project, and clinic design
- **CRITICAL:** Physician's PLLC signs lease directly (not MSO or Real Estate LLC)
- **Why This Model:**
  - MSO provides expertise without tying up capital in property ownership
  - Physician maintains flexibility to choose location and negotiate terms
  - Avoids MSO assuming property risk (vacancy, default)
  - PLLC can lease from any landlord (not restricted to MSO-affiliated real estate entity)
- **Advantage:** Physician gets real estate expertise and negotiating power without needing property knowledge

#### 3. Technology Platform (Fully Implemented)
- **EMR System:** DPC-optimized electronic medical record (e.g., Elation, Hint, Atlas)
- **Telehealth Platform:** HIPAA-compliant video visits and secure messaging
- **Patient Portal:** Online scheduling, results access, payment processing
- **Website & Digital Presence:** Professional website with Onevia branding
- **Cybersecurity:** Data backup, encryption, disaster recovery, HIPAA compliance monitoring
- **Advantage:** $50,000-$100,000+ in technology infrastructure provided from day one

#### 4. Brand License & Marketing
- **Brand Access:** Physician practices under established "Onevia" brand
- **Marketing Platform:** MSO provides marketing campaigns, patient acquisition strategies, social media management
- **Community Recognition:** Benefit from Onevia brand reputation and patient referrals
- **Advantage:** Instant credibility vs. unknown solo physician startup

#### 5. Legal & Compliance Infrastructure
- **Entity Formation:** MSO handles PLLC formation, operating agreements, EIN, state registrations
- **Legal Documents:** Patient membership agreements, employment contracts, HIPAA notices, consent forms
- **HIPAA Compliance:** Security risk assessments, policies, BAAs with vendors, breach response protocols
- **Regulatory Monitoring:** Track changes in state/federal healthcare regulations
- **Advantage:** $30,000-$60,000 in legal costs handled by MSO; physician focuses on medicine

#### 6. Administrative & Operational Support
- **HR & Payroll:** Centralized payroll processing, benefits administration, employee handbook
- **Accounting & Tax:** Bookkeeping, financial reporting, tax coordination with CPAs
- **Vendor Management:** Negotiate contracts with labs, imaging centers, medical suppliers
- **Practice Launch Support:** Physician onboarding, staff hiring assistance, operational training
- **Advantage:** No need to hire in-house admin staff; MSO handles backend operations

#### 7. Peer Network & Best Practice Sharing
- **Physician Community:** Join network of 10-20 Onevia-affiliated physicians
- **Collaborative Learning:** Share clinical protocols, operational workflows, patient success stories
- **Benchmarking:** Compare panel growth rates, patient satisfaction, financial performance
- **Mentorship:** Experienced Onevia physicians mentor new practice launches
- **Advantage:** Combat isolation; learn from peers; continuous improvement

## Division of Responsibilities: What MSO Provides vs. What Physicians Do

### What MSO Provides (The Platform)
Onevia MSO handles the high-capital, high-expertise barriers to entry so physicians can focus on medicine:

**Startup Phase:**
- Real estate sourcing and lease negotiation (PLLC signs lease directly)
- Startup capital loans: $300k-$400k (0% interest Years 1-2, then 7%)
- Technology platform implementation (EMR, telehealth, patient portal)
- Legal formation (PLLC, operating agreements, patient contracts)
- Brand licensing ("Onevia" trademark and marketing materials)
- Practice launch training (6-12 months intensive support)

**Ongoing Operations:**
- IT maintenance, cybersecurity, technology upgrades
- HR/payroll processing and benefits administration tools
- Accounting, bookkeeping, financial reporting, tax coordination
- HIPAA compliance (as Business Associate)
- Marketing platform and brand management
- Legal/regulatory monitoring
- Vendor relationship management (labs, suppliers, insurance)

### What Physicians Do (100% Operational Control)

**Clinical Operations (Physician's Exclusive Authority):**
- ALL medical care: diagnosis, treatment, prescribing, procedures
- Clinical protocols and standards of care
- Patient care delivery: in-person visits, telehealth, after-hours access
- Medical staff hiring and supervision (medical assistants, nurses)
- Quality improvement and patient safety initiatives
- Medical record documentation

**Business Operations (Physician's Practice, Physician's Decisions):**
- Day-to-day management: scheduling, workflow, office policies
- ALL hiring decisions (clinical + non-clinical staff) - MSO provides HR tools, physician makes final call
- Clinic buildout: layout, equipment, furniture, decor, patient experience design
- Local marketing execution: community networking, partnerships, personal brand
- Financial management: PLLC bank account, expense decisions, profit distributions
- Patient enrollment: building panel from 0 to 500-750 patients

**Strategic Decisions:**
- Practice culture and values
- Work schedule and hours per week
- Service mix within DPC model (chronic disease, sports medicine, pediatrics, etc.)
- Growth decisions: target panel size, hiring additional providers

### The Key Difference
- **MSO** = Removes $400k-$600k startup barrier + provides ongoing infrastructure
- **Physician** = Owns 100% of practice + controls all medical and business decisions
- **Result** = Physician practices medicine as independent entrepreneur without operational headaches

## The Physician's Commitment: 25% Revenue Share

In exchange for this comprehensive platform, the physician's PLLC pays Onevia MSO a **25% management fee** based on monthly membership revenue.

### Fee Calculation:
- **Example:** Physician achieves 600-patient panel × $100/month membership = $60,000/month revenue
- **MSO Fee:** $60,000 × 25% = $15,000/month ($180,000/year)
- **Physician Retains:** $45,000/month ($540,000/year) for physician salary, rent, staff, supplies, malpractice insurance

### Why 25% is Fair Market Value:

**MSO's Upfront Investment Per Practice:**
- Technology platform: $50,000-$100,000
- Brand development: $20,000-$50,000
- Legal & formation: $30,000-$60,000
- Startup capital loan: $300,000-$400,000 (at risk)
- Real estate facilitation: Ongoing support
- **Total MSO Investment:** $400,000-$600,000+ per practice

**MSO's Ongoing Costs (Regardless of Practice Performance):**
- IT infrastructure and cybersecurity maintenance
- Marketing platform and brand management
- Legal counsel and compliance monitoring
- HR and payroll systems
- MSO staff salaries (operations, IT, finance)

**MSO's Risk Exposure:**
- **Brand Risk:** Malpractice or scandal at one practice damages entire Onevia brand
- **Capital Risk:** Startup loans may not be repaid if practice fails
- **Platform Risk:** Fixed infrastructure costs even if practices underperform

**Industry Comparison:**
- **Healthcare Franchises:** 5-10% ongoing royalty PLUS $50k-$500k initial franchise fee
- **Dental Service Organizations (DSOs):** 20-40% management fees
- **Private Equity Healthcare:** 30-50%+ of EBITDA
- **Onevia:** 25% revenue share with NO initial franchise fee (MSO finances startup via loan)

**Economic Analysis:**
- At 600-patient panel, PLLC generates $720,000/year revenue
- MSO receives $180,000/year (25%)
- PLLC retains $540,000/year to cover:
  - Physician salary: $250,000
  - Medical assistant: $50,000
  - Rent to Real Estate LLC: $100,000
  - Malpractice insurance: $10,000
  - Medical supplies: $25,000
  - Loan repayment to MSO: $40,000-$60,000/year
  - Remaining: $45,000-$65,000 for additional staff, supplies, practice growth

**Result:** Physician earns $250,000/year salary (top quartile for primary care) while MSO recovers platform investment and generates sustainable cash flow to fund future practice launches.

## The Physician's Ownership: 100% Independence

**CRITICAL:** Each physician owns **100% of their own Professional Limited Liability Company (PLLC)**.

### What This Means:

**Full Clinical Autonomy:**
- Physician controls all medical decision-making (diagnosis, treatment, prescribing)
- Physician hires and supervises own clinical staff (medical assistants, nurses)
- Physician establishes clinical protocols and standards of care
- MSO has **ZERO** control over clinical decisions

**True Ownership:**
- Physician owns 100% of PLLC equity (MSO owns 0%)
- Physician receives 75% of practice revenue (after 25% MSO fee)
- Physician builds equity in practice over time
- Physician may exit Onevia platform (after 5-year initial MSA term) by:
  - Repaying MSO startup loan
  - Ceasing use of Onevia brand and IP
  - Finding new office space or negotiating continued lease

**Legal Structure:**
- Complies with Montana's corporate practice of medicine doctrine
- MSO is service provider, not practice owner
- Physician-ownership is gold standard for regulatory compliance
- No risk of private equity control or clinical interference

## Why the MSO Model is Superior to Alternatives

### Alternative 1: Single Large Practice (Traditional Group Model)
**Structure:** One large PLLC with multiple employed physicians

**Problems:**
- Physician employees (not owners) → less autonomy, less alignment
- Complex partnership structure if physicians become equity partners
- All eggs in one basket (single entity risk)
- Difficult to scale geographically (one clinic, one location)

**Onevia Advantage:** Multiple independent physician-owned practices → better alignment, lower risk, geographic scalability

---

### Alternative 2: Private Equity Roll-Up
**Structure:** PE firm acquires/consolidates physician practices; physicians become employees

**Problems:**
- PE firm controls practices → corporate practice of medicine risk
- Physicians lose autonomy; become employees focused on productivity
- PE exit timeline (5-7 years) misaligned with long-term patient care
- PE extracts 30-50%+ of profits → less goes to physicians

**Onevia Advantage:** Physicians own 100% of practices; MSO is service provider, not owner; 25% fee (not 50%); no forced exit timeline

---

### Alternative 3: Franchise Model
**Structure:** Onevia operates as traditional franchise (like fast food)

**Problems:**
- Upfront franchise fee ($50k-$500k) → high barrier to entry for physicians
- Ongoing royalty (5-10%) insufficient to cover MSO platform costs
- Franchisees must self-fund technology, brand development
- Less control over quality and brand consistency

**Onevia Advantage:** NO franchise fee; MSO finances startup via loan; 25% royalty includes comprehensive platform services; MSO maintains quality control through brand licensing and service agreements

---

### Alternative 4: Independent Practices (No MSO)
**Structure:** Each physician launches solo DPC practice independently

**Problems:**
- Physician must self-fund startup ($300k-$400k) → requires personal savings or bank loans
- Physician must build technology stack from scratch → months of work, steep learning curve
- Physician must develop brand and marketing independently → slow patient acquisition
- Physician isolated without peer network → limited best practice sharing

**Onevia Advantage:** MSO removes financial, operational, and marketing barriers; physician benefits from established platform; peer network for support and learning

## Risk Mitigation for MSO

Launching and supporting 10-20 practices requires MSO to manage several risks:

### 1. Physician Practice Failure Risk
**Risk:** Physician practice fails to achieve financial sustainability; MSO loses startup loan investment

**Mitigation:**
- **Rigorous Physician Selection:** Interview, background check, reference check, assessment of entrepreneurial mindset
- **Market Analysis:** Only launch practices in markets with sufficient demand (population, demographics, competition)
- **Intensive Support:** First 12 months of practice launch include weekly check-ins, operational troubleshooting, marketing support
- **Loan Security:** Personal guarantee from physician; collateral on PLLC assets
- **Performance Monitoring:** MSO tracks patient enrollment, revenue, satisfaction metrics; intervenes early if practice struggles

---

### 2. Brand Risk (Malpractice or Scandal)
**Risk:** One physician's malpractice or ethical violation damages entire Onevia brand

**Mitigation:**
- **Credentialing:** Verify medical licenses, DEA registration, malpractice history before affiliation
- **Quality Standards:** Management Services Agreement requires physicians to maintain professional liability insurance, comply with standards of care
- **Brand Protection:** MSO reserves right to terminate MSA for cause (malpractice, license suspension, ethical violations)
- **Separate Liability:** Each PLLC maintains own malpractice insurance; MSO not liable for physician clinical decisions
- **Communication Protocol:** Crisis management plan for responding to adverse events

---

### 3. Market Saturation Risk
**Risk:** Too many Onevia practices in one market cannibalize each other's patient base

**Mitigation:**
- **Geographic Spacing:** Launch practices in different neighborhoods or cities to avoid direct competition
- **Market Sizing:** Analyze population, demographics, competition before launching new practice
- **Panel Caps:** Physicians cap panels at 750 patients to preserve quality; practices don't compete for unlimited growth
- **Differentiation:** Each practice may develop specialty focus (pediatrics, women's health, sports medicine) to serve different patient segments

---

### 4. Technology Platform Failure Risk
**Risk:** EMR outage, data breach, or cybersecurity incident disrupts operations across all practices

**Mitigation:**
- **Vendor Selection:** Choose highly reliable, HIPAA-compliant technology vendors with 99.9%+ uptime
- **Redundancy:** Data backup and disaster recovery systems; failover capabilities
- **Cybersecurity:** Penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, employee training, cyber liability insurance
- **Vendor Contracts:** Service level agreements (SLAs) with vendors for rapid issue resolution

---

### 5. Regulatory Risk (Corporate Practice of Medicine)
**Risk:** State regulators challenge MSO-PLLC structure as violating corporate practice of medicine doctrine

**Mitigation:**
- **Legal Compliance:** Physician-ownership structure (100% PLLC ownership) is gold standard; MSO has zero clinical control
- **Legal Counsel:** Ongoing relationship with Montana healthcare attorney to monitor regulatory changes
- **Documentation:** Management Services Agreements explicitly prohibit MSO from controlling clinical decisions
- **Fair Market Value:** 25% management fee documented as FMV through independent valuation
- **Precedent:** MSO models widely used in healthcare (dental, ophthalmology, dermatology) and upheld by courts
